# 3. Competitive Analysis

## Competitive truth

“AI audit + material passport + matching + marketplace” is not globally new. Concular, Material Index, Audit Buddy and related platforms already cover substantial parts of that bundle. The pitch must never rely on claiming otherwise.

Indian recovered-material markets already exist for doors, windows, bricks and fixtures. The opportunity is not to “invent reuse” or displace informal actors. It is to give developers, contractors, buyers and recyclers a shared, earlier evidence layer. See the [CSE recovered-material report](https://www.cseindia.org/content/downloadreports/10325) and [CSE C&D plant assessment](https://www.cseindia.org/manage-construction-and-demolition-c-d-waste-better-to-fight-air-pollution-says-new-cse-assessment-12474).

## Defensible position

ReBuild Loop is **not**:

- Another empty marketplace requiring immediate network effects.
- A replacement for engineers, auditors, quantity surveyors or demolition contractors.
- A structural or hazardous-material certification system.
- A government compliance portal or EPR-certificate generator.
- A carbon-credit or verified-impact platform.

It is:

- The evidence and decision layer before those systems.
- Phone-first for low-data existing buildings.
- Confidence-aware and designed to ask for the next best evidence.
- Human-reviewed at every consequential boundary.
- Compatible with existing municipal, recycler, scrap and reuse channels.

## Long-term moat

The MVP itself is not a data moat. A credible moat can emerge from:

1. A labelled corpus of Indian building-component evidence and human corrections.
2. Disposition outcomes: accepted, rejected, downgraded, sold, reused or recycled.
3. Jurisdiction-specific recovery rules and partner requirements.
4. Verified local buyer/recycler capabilities, service areas and demand history.
5. Workflow trust with auditors, contractors and asset owners.

Do not claim this moat before the data and partnerships exist.
