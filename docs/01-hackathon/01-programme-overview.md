# Programme Overview and Published Tracks

## Executive summary

The **AI Agent Builder Series 2026** is presented as a 45-day national AI-agent innovation programme organised by **AI House Bangalore in collaboration with Google for Developers**. Participants build and submit a working AI agent, receive feedback, gather community votes, and compete on a national leaderboard.

The **Top 100 builders on the leaderboard** are shortlisted for an in-person,
full-day Grand Finale Hackathon in Bengaluru on **8 August 2026**. The current
finale listing does not disclose the exact venue.

This document records the programme information rechecked on 30 July 2026.
Rules and schedules may change, so important decisions should be checked
against the official programme page and submission platform.

## Eligibility

The programme page states that the following people from across India may participate:

- Developers
- Students
- Founders
- Researchers
- Builders at any skill level who want to create real-world AI agents

The public page does not clearly specify whether entries must be individual or may be submitted as teams. It also does not state a maximum team size.

## Problem tracks

Participants may choose one of the defined challenges or use the open-innovation track.

### 1. Rural health worker attendance

ASHA and ANM household visits are often recorded on paper. Supervisors may be unable to verify visits, identify inaccurate reporting, or see underserved areas in real time.

Possible agent capabilities:

- Log and verify household visits
- Detect suspicious or incomplete reporting
- Prioritise underserved locations
- Alert supervisors about missed visits
- Generate PHC-level operational summaries

### 2. Waste collection and bin overflow

Fixed collection schedules can result in overflowing bins, missed pickups, inefficient routes, and limited visibility for municipal teams.

Possible agent capabilities:

- Accept citizen reports and images
- Classify urgency and identify locations
- Detect missed collections
- Optimise vehicle routes
- Notify responsible teams
- Track an incident until resolution

### 3. Traffic enforcement and congestion

Traffic violations may go undetected, signals may use fixed timings, and authorities may lack a unified system for congestion management and emergency-vehicle priority.

Possible agent capabilities:

- Detect or triage violations
- Analyse congestion patterns
- Recommend adaptive signal changes
- Prioritise emergency vehicles
- Coordinate alerts across responsible teams
- Produce operational dashboards and reports

### 4. Open innovation

Participants may bring their own challenge and build an AI agent for a real problem affecting a city, campus, organisation, or community.

This track provides the greatest freedom, but the submission should still demonstrate a narrow, credible problem, identifiable users, real actions, and measurable outcomes.
